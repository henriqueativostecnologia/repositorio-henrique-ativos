# Establish conventions for your team

Setting up team conventions in Asana helps everyone work together more effectively. When your team follows consistent practices, you'll reduce confusion, improve collaboration, and make your work easier to find and understand.

## Task Conventions

### Naming tasks

- Start task names with action verbs (e.g., "Review budget proposal" instead of "Budget proposal")
- Keep names clear and actionable so anyone can understand what needs to be done
- Avoid vague titles like "Follow up" or "Meeting"

### Writing task descriptions

A task description should give the assignee all the necessary information to complete the task. Here are some tips:

- Link to relevant work in the task description by [@mentioning](https://help.asana.com/s/article/text-formatting-in-asana?language=en_US) related people, tasks, projects, or messages.
- Add links and [attachments](https://help.asana.com/s/article/task-comments-and-attachments?language=en_US) to include external work, centralizing information.
- Mark a task as [dependent](https://asana.com/guide/help/tasks/dependencies) upon another, so teammates start that task when the prior task is completed.
- Use [rich text](https://asana.com/guide/help/fundamentals/text#gl-rich-text) in task descriptions to clarify your message with formatted text and lists.

### Setting due dates

Set realistic [due dates](https://help.asana.com/s/article/scheduling-tasks-using-start-dates-due-dates-and-times?language=en_US?language=en_US) for your teammates as soon as they are known, or add an estimated due date as a rough guide. Due dates ensure work gets prioritized, ensuring deadlines are met.

Add [start dates](https://asana.com/guide/help/tasks/fields#sts=Task%20due%20dates,%20due%20times,%20and%20date%20ranges) so it’s clear when someone should start on the work to complete it by the deadline successfully.

### Comments and updates

- Add teammates and relevant stakeholders as [collaborators](https://asana.com/guide/help/tasks/understanding-tasks#gl-collaborators) so they can stay up to date on task progress.
- Communicate within task comments to keep conversations connected to the work
- Use project messages for important updates that affect all project members
- Replace "send me an email" with "assign me a task" for better tracking

### Updating tasks assigned to you

When someone assigns you a task, you are in charge of moving that work forward.

- [Like the task](https://asana.com/guide/help/tasks/fields#gl-likes) so the requester knows you have seen it.
- If you have questions or updates, [comment](https://asana.com/guide/help/tasks/comments) directly on the task.
- If you change the due date, add a comment to explain why.
- [Pin](https://help.asana.com/s/article/task-comments-and-attachments?language=en_US) the most relevant comment to the top; this makes it easier to find, without reading through every comment.
- If you don’t have the bandwidth to take on the work, assign the task back to the creator or use [@mentioning](https://help.asana.com/s/article/text-formatting-in-asana?language=en_US) to ask teammates if they can take over it.
- If the task has [custom fields](https://help.asana.com/s/article/custom-fields?language=en_US), fill them out accordingly and continue to update them as work progresses.

### Naming template tasks

When you create templates, name the task or project appropriately so it’s clear to teammates that it is a template (e.g., “Template” or “Duplicate me”). This also makes the task or project easier to locate through [search](https://help.asana.com/s/article/search-and-search-views?language=en_US).

## Project conventions

[Projects](https://help.asana.com/s/article/understanding-projects?language=en_US) allow you to organize all tasks related to a specific initiative, goal, or significant work in one place. Similar to tasks, [anyone can create a project.](https://help.asana.com/s/article/create-projects-in-asana?language=en_US)

### Naming projects

- Use descriptive names that clearly identify the work
- Include timeframes for time-bound projects (e.g., "Q1 2025 product launch" instead of "Product launch")
- Consider department or team prefixes for better organization

### Project setup

- Create projects in the team where most of the work happens
- Make projects public by default unless they contain sensitive information
- Set up the [project overview](https://asana.com/guide/help/projects/project-overview)[ ](https://asana.com/guide/help/projects/project-overview)so your teammates can always find the project brief and see project roles
- Decide who can add people to projects and establish guidelines

### Project structure

- Choose appropriate project views (list, board, timeline, calendar) based on the work type
- Use [sections](https://asana.com/guide/help/projects/basics#gl-sections) to organize tasks within projects
- Leverage milestones to mark important progress points
- Connect related projects using portfolios
- Create a [custom template](https://asana.com/guide/help/projects/project-templates-2.0#gl-customizing-your-template) or use an [Asana created template](https://asana.com/templates) to standardize common workflows and projects. [Templates](https://help.asana.com/s/article/create-projects-quickly-with-templates?language=en_US) help get projects off to a quick start and ensure you haven’t missed any vital steps.

### Adding custom fields

Set up [custom fields](https://asana.com/guide/help/premium/custom-fields#gl-manage) so you can track all necessary information on every task created in the project.

- Use organization-wide custom fields for consistency
- Common useful fields include priority, department, and progress status
- Save frequently used fields to your custom field library

### Sending status updates

Status updates clarify how the project progresses. The project owner or dedicated teammate should send regular [project status updates](https://help.asana.com/s/article/communicating-in-asana?language=en_US).

- Set expectations for regular project status updates
- Decide who should provide updates and how often
- Use status updates to keep stakeholders informed and reduce progress questions

### Completing and archiving projects

[Completed projects](https://help.asana.com/s/article/project-progress-and-status-updates?language=en_US) should be marked as **Complete** in the status update. We recommend completing projects before archiving. Archiving is only possible for [project admins](https://help.asana.com/s/article/project-permissions?language=en_US).

### Managing a project

When you create a project, you become the [project owner ](https://asana.com/guide/help/projects/basics#gl-owner)by default. You can change the project owner in the progress view.

- As new tasks are created, ensure tasks have an assignee and due date, move them to the appropriate sections, and update their custom fields.
- [Add project rules](https://asana.com/guide/help/premium/rules) to automate workflows and save time.
- Set [milestones](https://asana.com/guide/help/tasks/actions#gl-milestones) to keep tabs on key markers of progress.
- If you manage multiple projects, add them to a portfolio to organize and monitor them.

## Email conventions

### Using Messages

We recommend using [messages](https://help.asana.com/s/article/communicating-in-asana?language=en_US) to communicate within Asana with your colleagues and easily link to relevant work instead of email for all internal communication. Gently remind your teammates to use Asana instead of email when necessary.

### Using email notifications

Asana sends [email notifications](https://asana.com/guide/help/email/email-from-asana) [](https://asana.com/guide/help/email/email-from-asana) for new activities so that teammates who aren’t using Asana all the time can stay in the loop.

We suggest keeping email notifications on until you and your teammates are accustomed to checking Asana often. You can [turn them on and off](https://asana.com/guide/help/email/email-from-asana) via **My Settings.**

### Reducing email within your team

Email is great for reaching someone outside your organization, but it’s not equipped to plan, manage, and prioritize work.

With our [Asana for Gmail Add-on](https://asana.com/guide/help/api/gmail-add-on) and [Asana for Outlook app](https://asana.com/apps/outlook), you can quickly turn emails into action right from your email inbox. Using these email integrations can be an easy way to get your team into the habit of moving work from email into Asana without changing how they work.

### Slack + Asana

You can easily take action on tasks from Slack using the [Slack + Asana](https://help.asana.com/s/article/slack-and-asana?language=en_US) integration without switching tools constantly.

## Setting up your conventions

### Step 1: Gather input

- Meet with your team to discuss current pain points
- Identify areas where inconsistency causes problems
- Get buy-in from team members who will be using these conventions

### Step 2: Document your decisions

- Create a project to store your team conventions
- Make it easily accessible to all team members
- Include examples of good and poor practices
- Add new team members to this project during onboarding

### Step 3: Communicate and train

- Share conventions with your entire team
- Provide training on new practices
- Create quick reference guides for common scenarios
- Address questions and concerns as they arise

### Step 4: Enforce and iterate

- Regularly review how well conventions are working
- Make adjustments based on team feedback
- Celebrate when team members follow conventions well
- Gently redirect when conventions aren't being followed

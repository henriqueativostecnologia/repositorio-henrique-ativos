# Using Asana for group projects

Working on group projects in any educational environment requires effective collaboration, clear communication, and organized task management - or said another way, who is doing what by when. Asana provides the tools needed to keep your group projects on track and ensure everyone stays aligned until the project is complete.

## Get set up

The first thing to do is to [create a project](https://help.asana.com/s/article/how-to-create-a-project). Click the **+ Create** button in the top left hand corner of your page, and select **Blank project** or choose from the list of templates if they are useful. Decide on a name for your project, and then invite your classmates.

## Create and organize your tasks

Next, break your project down into specific [tasks](https://help.asana.com/s/article/understanding-tasks). If you’re using [list view](https://help.asana.com/s/article/list-view) - which is the simplest way to view your tasks - type each task on a new line. You can use [subtasks](https://help.asana.com/s/article/subtasks) to break work down into smaller chunks, but for clarity, it may be more beneficial to stick with tasks in the early stages, especially when working with those who haven’t used Asana before.

Once you’ve decided on the rough outline of your project, you’ll need to [assign the tasks](https://help.asana.com/s/article/assign-tasks-to-teammates) to the right people. Remember, you can only assign a task to one person in Asana - this ensures clear ownership and accountability. Click the assignee field on the task and type the name of the person who will be responsible for the task.

Next, you’ll need to choose [due dates](https://help.asana.com/s/article/scheduling-tasks-using-start-dates-due-dates-and-times) for your tasks. Due dates help to keep people accountable, so that work gets done on time and everyone’s piece of the puzzle fits together in the end. In the early stages, it’s fine to go with your best guess, and you can always change due dates around depending on bandwidth or anything else that comes up.

## Communicate and collaborate

There’s bound to be plenty of discussion during a group project. Instead of relying on email threads or text messages outside of Asana, communicate where the work is happening, directly on the task. You can add [comments](https://help.asana.com/s/article/task-comments-and-attachments) to ask questions, provide updates or feedback, and even use celebration stickers to show your appreciation. 

By communicating directly on tasks, your group ensures that none of the communication gets lost in a thread or an email, and everyone is clear on the direction the work is taking. @mention your classmates to bring them into the discussion by adding them as a [collaborator](https://help.asana.com/s/article/collaborating-in-asana#h_01HEMMRVCMDMCZ78Y1H9BAB50G), even if they’re not the task assignee. You can even add reference material or documents as files directly on the task, too.

## File management

As mentioned, you can add files directly to tasks to ensure everyone has the context they need. What’s more, any [files attached](https://help.asana.com/s/article/task-comments-and-attachments#gl-attachments) to tasks in the project are also collected in the [files view](https://help.asana.com/s/article/files-view), so for a group project you can still find all the relevant material from the entire scope of the work being done, even if you’re not involved in the specific task that the file was attached to.

## Timeline management

Use [timeline view](https://help.asana.com/s/article/timeline) to visualize your work compared to the project deadline. A visual representation of the work being done can help you decide if you need to allocate more time to a particular task, bring some of the work forward, or reassign tasks to ensure that work is spread evenly across the group.

[Milestones](https://help.asana.com/s/article/task-actions#gl-actions-list) are a great way to track how work is progressing. Use milestones to track completion of certain phases of work, or to show when the biggest blockers have been taken care of.

## Tips for success

To make the most of Asana for your group projects:

- Keep task descriptions clear and specific - This helps everyone understand exactly what needs to be done.
- Communicate regularly - Keep your team informed of progress and any potential blockers, using comments or project messages.
- Use due dates consistently - This ensures work stays on track and task dependencies are managed effectively.
- Keep files organized and properly labeled - This makes it easy for everyone to find what they need inside the project.

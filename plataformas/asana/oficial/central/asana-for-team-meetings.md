# Asana for team meetings

Running productive team meetings requires clear notes and effective follow-up. With AI Meetings, Asana transcribes your meetings, summarizes the discussion, and suggests action items you can turn into tasks, so decisions don’t get lost after the call. Here’s how to set up AI Meetings, capture your team meetings, and follow up on action items in Asana.

## Use AI Meetings to capture action items

[AI Meetings](https://help.asana.com/s/article/asana-meetings) connects the conversations where work gets discussed to the tasks that follow. Instead of meeting recaps living in a separate tool, Asana transcribes your meeting and generates an AI summary and suggested action items. You can turn those suggestions into tasks without leaving Asana. AI Meetings is available on Advanced, Enterprise, and Enterprise+ plans. Generating a summary and suggested tasks uses one AI Request for meetings up to 60 minutes, plus one for each additional hour or part of an hour, with a 5-minute grace period.

## Set up AI Meetings

Start by connecting your calendar. In Asana, navigate to **Meetings** in the sidebar under **Knowledge**, select **Connect calendar** or **Get started** from the top banner, and follow the prompts to authenticate your Google Calendar or Outlook Calendar account. You can connect one calendar, and a calendar is required to use AI Meetings.

Next, choose how you want to record and transcribe your meetings. Asana Desktop captures audio from your device for Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams meetings, and no bot joins your call. It requires macOS 13.0 or later on Apple Silicon, or 64-bit Windows 10 or later. Download Asana Desktop from the setup screen, then turn on notifications for it in your system settings so you’re alerted when a meeting is starting. On macOS, Asana also asks for microphone, system audio, and accessibility permissions. The Zoom integration works only for Zoom meetings you host; to set it up, select **Connect Zoom** from the setup screen. For supported browsers and meeting apps, see [Troubleshooting AI Meetings](https://help.asana.com/s/article/troubleshooting-asana-meetings).

## Using AI Meetings

### Record and transcribe a meeting

To transcribe a meeting, its calendar event must include a Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams link. With Asana Desktop, any attendee can transcribe, including meetings hosted outside your organization. Navigate to **Meetings** in the sidebar, open the **Home** tab, and select **Start** for any meeting that begins within the next five minutes. Asana opens your call and shows a recording control at the top left of your screen. You can also start from a desktop notification or from the calendar icon in the Asana Desktop mini app.

To transcribe a Zoom meeting with the Zoom integration, start a cloud recording as the meeting host. Asana retrieves the transcript after the recording ends, which can take up to 30 minutes. 

AI Meetings does not begin transcribing automatically. A consent reminder appears in the meeting notification before recording starts. When transcription starts, Asana also posts a message in the meeting chat from your account.

### Share meeting notes

Transcripts, summaries, and action items are private by default. Before, during, or after the meeting, you can add collaborators or connect the meeting to one or more projects or teamspaces. Anyone you add gains access to the entire meeting, including the summary, transcript, and action items. Connected meetings appear in the project’s **AI Meetings** tab, so notes sit next to the related work.

You can share meetings with specific people only. Team-wide, domain-wide, and public sharing aren’t supported, and guests and view-only users can’t be given access. To set a default sharing setting, select the gear icon at the top right of the **Meetings** page.

## Post-meeting follow-up

### Review action items

When your summary and action items are ready, you’ll get a notification in your Asana inbox. Summaries are usually ready within minutes with Asana Desktop, while Zoom transcripts can take longer. Open the meeting to review the AI-generated summary, which any meeting member can edit. Select **Show more** to see the full transcript, which can’t be edited.

Suggested tasks appear at the bottom of the meeting. Edit a suggestion if needed, then accept it to create an Asana task, or decline it to remove it. Before you accept, make sure each task is clearly defined, assigned to the right team member, and has an appropriate due date. To return to a past meeting, navigate to **Meetings** and select the **Previous meetings** tab.

### Track progress

Utilize Asana’s [dashboards](https://help.asana.com/s/article/project-dashboards) to track the progress of action items. This visibility helps the team stay accountable and ensures that tasks are completed on time.

### Generate reports

Create custom [reports](https://help.asana.com/s/article/reporting-with-dashboards) to review meeting outcomes and monitor the completion of action items. These reports can be used in subsequent meetings to ensure progress is being made and to discuss any blockers.

## Benefits of using Asana for weekly team meetings

By using Asana to organize your weekly team meetings, you’ll see improvements in the following areas:

- **Automatic meeting notes**: AI Meetings transcribes your calls and generates a summary, so no one has to take notes by hand.
- **Fewer missed action items**: Suggested tasks come straight from the conversation, and you can edit and accept them as Asana tasks without leaving the meeting record.
- **Context from your work**: Summaries draw on your Work Graph® and can link to relevant tasks, comments, status updates, and people.
- **Centralized information**: Connecting meetings to projects keeps transcripts, summaries, and tasks next to the work they relate to.
- **Reliable task tracking**: Dashboards and reports provide visibility into task progress, helping the team follow through on meeting action items.

By using AI Meetings alongside Asana’s project tools, you can make sure meeting decisions are captured, shared with the right people, and turned into tracked work. This approach leads to more productive meetings, better outcomes, and improved team alignment.

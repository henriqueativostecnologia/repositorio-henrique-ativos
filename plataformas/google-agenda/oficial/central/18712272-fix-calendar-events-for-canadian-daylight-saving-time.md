# Fix Calendar events for Canadian daylight saving time

Several Canadian provinces and territories recently decided to stay on daylight saving time permanently. As a result, some Google Calendar events created in certain time zones in Canada may appear one hour late on and after November 1, 2026. This article explains which events are affected by the change and how to update them.

## Learn which Google Calendar events are affected

Your event may be one hour late if its time zone is in:

- Alberta
- British Columbia
- Manitoba
- The Northwest Territories

Affected events have a warning badge warning badge on your calendar grid. When you open an event you organized, you get a banner that allows you to move the event one hour earlier.

## Learn how Google Calendar fixes your events

In October 2026, Google Calendar will automatically shift most affected events back by one hour to their original scheduled local times. This automatic fix applies to:

- Single events
- Recurring events that don’t have future exceptions, such as a single rescheduled instance, a different room, or a custom description

After the events move to their original times, your guests will need to RSVP to your event again. To track these time zone corrections across the calendars you manage, you can enable email notifications for changed events. [Learn how to change Google Calendar notifications](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37242).

Google Calendar won’t be able to update all affected events. If you already corrected an event’s time, it won’t be moved again by Google.

## Fix individual events on your calendar

**Important:**

- In-product banners and one-click fixes only appear on the web version of Calendar. They don’t appear in the Calendar app for Android, iPhone, and iPad Calendar or third-party calendar apps.
- To change the event time, you must be the organizer or have at least "[Make changes (see private events as free/busy)](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/15716974)" access to the organizer's calendar.

1.  On your computer, open [Google Calendar](https://calendar.google.com/).
2.  Open an affected event.
3.  To move the event, click **Move one hour earlier**.
    - Or, to dismiss the warning, click **Keep current time**.

**Tips:**

- After you fix the event on the web, discrepancies can still appear in Microsoft Exchange and other third-party apps.
- For the Google Calendar app Calendar :
  - Make sure the app is on its latest version.
  - Check that your mobile operating system is on its latest version.
  - If you still notice discrepancies between the app and Google Calendar on the web, temporarily change your calendar to a matching Greenwich Mean Time zone. [Learn how to use Google Calendar in different time zones](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37064).

## Fix recurring events on your calendar

**Important**: Custom changes to recurring events, like attachments or different guests, can be lost. This happens if you apply time changes to **All events** or **This and following events**. To avoid this issue, follow these steps.

### Step 1: Update the time for an upcoming event

1.  On your calendar, open the next event in the series scheduled after November 1, 2026.
2.  At the top of the event, click **Move one hour earlier**.
3.  When prompted, select **This and following events**.

### Step 2: Recreate any custom changes for an upcoming event

1.  On your calendar, open a future event in the series you want to apply a custom change to.
2.  Add the custom change, such as a different guest or an attachment.
3.  Click **Save**.

**Tip:** After you update an event, check that it has a conference room and attendees have no scheduling conflicts for the new time.

## Manage meetings from others or on secondary calendars

If you are an invited guest to a meeting, or have a shared calendar, how you should handle the time change depends on the organizer:

- **Meetings organized by a Google Calendar organizer:**
  - If you are an invited guest, the organizer is responsible for the meeting.
  - You will not find in-product tools to directly fix the event. Google's automated tools address only events owned by the organizer.
  - If you have concerns about the displayed time, reach out to the organizer.
- **Meetings organized by a Microsoft Outlook organizer:**
  - An Outlook organizer might get the correct time. However, your Google Calendar version might appear incorrect due to system differences.
  - For any meeting from an Outlook organizer, whether it's an individual event or a recurring series, you should always check with the organizer.
  - If the time is incorrect, ask the organizer to resend the invitation with the updated time. The organizer should not forward the event, as this does not fix the time.
- **Meetings on secondary calendars:**
  - You only get a warning for secondary calendar events if you have at least “Make changes (see private events as free/busy)” access to that calendar.

## Manage room bookings

As a result of these changes, room conflicts are likely to occur when your events shift. For example:

- In May 2026, Team A booked a room for 9:00 AM on November 4, 2026 before time zone updates. The booking currently displays 10:00 AM.
- Team B then booked the same room for 9:00 AM using updated time zone definitions. If you move Team A's event back to 9:00 AM, it could cause the room to decline the shifted event.

When you update an event’s time, the booked room is released, and Google Calendar attempts to rebook it automatically for the new time. If you organized the event, you get an email when a room declines an event. After you update the time, check that your conference room is still accepted and available. [Learn more about why a room might decline an event](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/16107253).

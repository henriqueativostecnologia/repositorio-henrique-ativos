### [Build your prompt with more dimensions](https://www.notion.com/help/guides/how-to-get-better-answers-from-notion-ai#build-your-prompt-with-more-dimensions)

Instead of treating your conversation with Notion AI like a simple keyword hunt, think of it as piecing together a puzzle where each detail helps create a clearer picture. When you approach your searches with multiple dimensions in mind, you're essentially giving Notion AI a richer context to work with. It's like the difference between asking someone to find *"a book"* versus asking them to find *"the red hardcover novel you read last summer."* The more dimensions you provide, the more precise your results will be.

**Four dimensions that help narrow your results with Notion AI:**

- **State your topic—**Instead of a vague wording, think about the specific subject matter. For example, rather than just searching for *"sales numbers,"* try *"Q1 2025 APAC sales performance analysis."* The more specific you are about the topic, the more likely you are to find exactly what you're looking for.

Be specific about document titles and use relevant keywords to help Notion AI find the resources you need.

- **Identify the author—** Finding documents is easier when you know who created them. Instead of searching broadly for *"team update,"* try *"team update by Alex.*" For example, try *"Can you find Sandra’s brand guidelines document?"*

Mention the author's name in your prompt to Notion to quickly spot relevant documents.

- **Add time context**— While Notion AI can only see when items were last edited (not when they were created), adding time context helps narrow down results. Instead of searching broadly for *"marketing report,"* try *"Sally’s marketing report from last quarter."* This is especially helpful when you're looking for recent updates or specific versions of documents.

Mention specific time periods such as days, dates, quarters, months and years in your prompt to help Notion AI find the most relevant document.

- **Specify your document type—** Help Notion AI understand what format you're looking for. Is it a page, database, wiki page, or something else? For example, instead of just asking "*Where's the customer feedback?",* try *"Can you find the customer feedback database that Stephanie created to track NPS scores for the marketing team last month?"*

Specify what kind of document you're looking for—whether it's a database, wiki, or meeting notes.

## [What to do when you're not getting the results you want](https://www.notion.com/help/guides/how-to-get-better-answers-from-notion-ai#what-to-do-when-you're-not-getting-the-results-you-want)

Sometimes you might not get exactly what you're looking for, even with well-crafted prompts (and that's totally okay!). The key is knowing how to adjust your approach when your first try doesn't quite hit the mark.

Here are five practical strategies to help you troubleshoot and refine your prompts:

- **Build better results through iteration—** Notion AI remembers context throughout your conversation. Rather than starting over with each prompt, you'll get the best results by treating it as an interactive tool—asking follow-up questions, refining your requests, and guiding the conversation toward exactly what you need. For example, try saying *"That's helpful, but could you focus specifically on the marketing aspects?"*

  to iterate results.

Rephrase your prompts to refine your search results. Ask questions in multiple ways to get better answers from Notion AI.

Double check the little details

- **Adjust your scope by choosing your source—**If you're seeing too many or too few results, adjust your search scope. Add or remove specific details like timeframes, team members, or project information in your prompt. You can also use the source picker to control which information sources to search.

Use the source picker to easily filter which sources Notion AI looks through when searching.

Use page verification for more trusted results

- **Write like you talk—** Write naturally, as if you're having a casual conversation. Instead of *"locate document,"* try something like *"Can you help me find Sarah's product roadmap from last quarter?".* Sometimes rephrasing your request—just like when you're explaining something to someone who didn't understand the first time—can help you get better results.

Notion AI works best with conversational language— just like how you'd naturally explain something to a coworker.

- **Modify your timeframe—** By default, Notion AI often limits its search to recent content from a specific lookback window. To search through older content, make sure to specify a broader time range in your request. For example, rather than simply asking "find the marketing plan," try "find all marketing plans from 2023 and 2024" to include older documents in your search results.

Set your Notion workspace up for AI success

- **Make it clear you want to search—** Sometimes Notion AI might think you're asking for general knowledge instead of searching your workspace. To keep things focused, either use the scope selector or just mention that you want to search your workspace content. For example, *"Please find any documents in our workspace about the new employee onboarding process"* is better than *"How does employee onboarding work?"*

Use the source picker to select specific documents so Notion AI can surface answers based on their context.
